Why there are no numbers here yet

Demo prices on the dashboard are simulated so you can practise safely. Company facts cannot be simulated honestly: a made-up P/E or a fake headline would teach you the wrong thing. So this page stays empty until a real source is connected, and explains the vocabulary meanwhile.

  1. Create a free account at Finnhub (best fit: profile, stats, earnings and headlines, 60 requests a minute) or Alpha Vantage (profile and stats; headlines and earnings on request, 25 requests a day).
  2. Copy the API key. On the Dashboard, tap Live data, paste it under the provider, test, save.
  3. Come back here and look up any US ticker. Your key is stored only in this browser (localStorage) and sent only to that provider.

Reading the numbers

How traders read a news page

  1. Source and time first. Wire services report facts fast. A headline from three days ago is already in the price.
  2. Is it new, and is it material? A scheduled product launch rarely moves a stock. A surprise guidance cut does.
  3. Check the calendar. Earnings dates are known in advance. Gaps through a stop-loss are the main way a small loss becomes a big one.
  4. Compare with the market. If SPY fell 2% too, the "company news" may be a coincidence.
  5. Valuation as context, not a signal. P/E tells you what the market expects; the chart tells you what it is doing. Neither predicts tomorrow.
  6. Write it down. Note your explanation in the Journal before you see what happens next, so you can grade yourself later.
