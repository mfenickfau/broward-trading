Equity curve

—

Your equity curve appears here once your account has been marked to market a few times. Place a trade on the dashboard to begin.

Each point is your equity at the time positions were marked. The dashed line is your starting cash: above it you are up, below it you are down. A smooth upward line beats a jagged one that ends in the same place; that is what the Sharpe ratio and max drawdown measure.

Payoff at expiration

Green area: profit at expiration for that stock price. Red: loss. Vertical markers show your breakevens and the current spot. The dotted curve is what the position is worth today under the model: notice how it is smoother than the expiration payoff, and how it drifts toward the kinked line as days pass (that drift is theta). The shaded bands are the probability range: under the model the stock finishes inside the darker band about 68% of the time and inside the lighter band about 95% of the time.

Simulation settings

Make the paper account behave more like a real brokerage. Everything here applies to the active account (Main) and only to new fills.

Instant fills the whole order at once. Volume-limited fills at most 10% of a bar's volume per bar and leaves the rest working, which is how a big order in a thinly traded stock really behaves.

Market orders fill a little worse than the quote: 10 bps is 0.10%, or 10 cents on a $100 stock. Real slippage is larger in fast markets and in small stocks.

Most US apps charge $0 for stocks (they earn from order flow and the spread instead). Per-share pricing, around half a cent a share, is common at brokers for active traders; flat per-trade fees are older-style pricing that punishes small, frequent trades.

Dividends

Shareholders of dividend payers receive cash each quarter; short sellers pay it. Uses the demo data's dividend schedule.

Idle cash earns interest, accrued daily. It is the return you get for doing nothing, a fair bar for any strategy to beat.

Pattern day trader rule

In a US margin account under $25,000, a fourth day trade (open and close the same day) within five business days is blocked. Turn it on to practise within the limits a real small account faces.

Short sellers borrow the shares and pay a fee for every day the position is open. Easy-to-borrow stocks cost well under 1%; heavily shorted ones can cost 20% or more.

About this simulator

  • Market orders fill at the current quote; limit and stop orders fill when a later quote reaches them, with realistic gap handling.
  • Short sales require 150 % of the exposure in cash, like a margin account; writing options adds 20 % of the strike notional.
  • Option positions are valued with Black-Scholes from the underlying's quote and the Lab's base volatility, and go to intrinsic value at expiry.
  • Everything is stored in this browser only (localStorage key bt.portfolio). Clearing site data clears the account.
