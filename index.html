          Open orders

          Limit and stop orders rest here and fill against each new bar or tick, exactly like a broker would fill them against the tape: buy limits need the low to reach your price, buy stops need the high to reach it.

          Alerts

          Alerts are checked on every price tick while the dashboard is open, and watchlist symbols are checked with each quote refresh. Price alerts draw a dotted line on the chart. They are a study tool: an alert tells you a level was reached, never that you must trade it.

          Moving averages (SMA / EMA)

          The average closing price over N bars. SMA 20 above SMA 50 means the short-term trend is stronger than the long-term one (a “golden cross” when it crosses up, “death cross” when down). EMA weights recent bars more, so it reacts faster; EMA 9/21 is a popular fast pair for swing trades.

          RSI (Relative Strength Index)

          Momentum from 0 to 100. Under 30 is “oversold” (sellers may be exhausted), over 70 is “overbought”. Traders look for RSI to turn back from these zones, ideally with a divergence against price.

          MACD

          Difference between the 12 and 26 EMA, with a 9-bar signal line. The histogram is MACD minus signal. Histogram turning positive is a bullish cross; negative is bearish.

          Bollinger Bands

          SMA 20 plus and minus two standard deviations. Price touching the lower band is statistically stretched down; the upper band, stretched up. Narrow bands (a “squeeze”) often precede big moves.

          VWAP

          The average price paid today, weighted by volume. Institutions benchmark fills against it: price above VWAP means buyers are paying up; below, sellers are in control. Resets every session on intraday charts.

          Ichimoku cloud

          Five lines in one glance. Price above the cloud is an uptrend, below is a downtrend, inside is no-man’s land. Tenkan (9) crossing above Kijun (26) is a bullish trigger, and a thick cloud ahead marks strong future support or resistance.

          Fibonacci retracements

          After a swing from low to high, pullbacks often pause at 38.2 %, 50 % and 61.8 % of the move. The levels are drawn from the highest high and lowest low of the last 120 bars. They are zones to watch, not magic numbers.

          Stochastic, ADX, OBV, ATR

          Stochastic shows where the close sits in the recent range (above 80 high, below 20 low). ADX measures trend strength regardless of direction: above 25 trust crossovers, below 20 trust mean reversion. OBV adds volume on up bars and subtracts it on down bars; it should confirm price. ATR is the typical bar range, useful for sizing stops (e.g. 2 × ATR below entry).

          Candlestick patterns

          Single bars and small groups that hint at reversals: a hammer after a decline, a shooting star after a rally, engulfing bars that swallow the previous one, morning/evening stars, and three soldiers or crows in a row. Turn on “Patterns” and hover a marker.

          Support, resistance and trend lines

          Dashed lines mark recent swing lows (support) and swing highs (resistance), where price reversed before. Use ✎ Trend line to connect two swing points yourself: an upward line of higher lows is an uptrend. Your lines are saved per symbol.

          Order types

          Market fills now at the current price. Limit fills only at your price or better (buy lower, sell higher). Stop becomes a market order once price reaches your trigger: used to cut losses or enter breakouts. Stop-limit triggers like a stop but then insists on a limit, so it may not fill in a fast gap.
