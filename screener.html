        How to use a screener (and what it cannot do)

        A screener is a filter, not a forecast. It answers "which names look like this today?" so you can spend your time on a short list instead of scrolling through every chart.

        • Start broad, then tighten. Each extra filter removes names. If a scan returns nothing, loosen one threshold rather than stacking more rules.
        • Open the chart. A matching number can hide a gap, an earnings surprise or a thin market. Read the price action and volume before you form a view.
        • Indicators lag. Moving-average crosses and 52-week highs confirm what already happened. They describe the past; none of them knows what happens next.
        • Practise first. Paper-trade or backtest a setup many times, with a stop and a position size, before you trust it. Most simple setups do not beat buying and holding after costs.
        • Demo prices are simulated. In demo mode every symbol follows a made-up random walk, so matches are for practice only. With a live data key the scan uses real daily bars, paced to the free plan's rate limits.
